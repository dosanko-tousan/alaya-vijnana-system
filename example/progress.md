# Progress — distilled example

> The author's real `progress` file with **individual karma distilled away**. This is the *most heavily distilled* file in the example, because the live version carries the most sensitive individual seeds: a confidential disclosure to a company, an ongoing legal matter, specific grant and publication identifiers, and detailed readership statistics. All removed.
>
> What remains is the **structure**: what a mid-term memory file tracks, and how it is run. (Original kept privately.)

---

## What this file holds

Mid-term memory — the flow of karmic ripening (業の異熟) over weeks to months. Work in progress, deadlines, the visible movement of research and writing. Distinct from the always-on memory slots (which hold live insight, not status) and from the long-term files.

---

## What it tracks (categories, not specifics)

In the live system this file tracks, in full detail:

- grant applications — status, amounts, deadlines, what each is waiting on
- a paper's progress through journal submission
- publications across several venues (alignment essays, memoir, native-language posts)
- a responsible-disclosure process with a company — *kept confidential, removed entirely here*
- an ongoing legal matter
- collaborators and their status
- planned next applications, not yet active

For this public version, every specific — names, URLs, amounts, case numbers, article titles, traffic numbers — is removed. They are precisely the individual karma the method evaporates. What a reader needs to see is *that the file tracks these categories, and keeps them out of memory* — not their contents.

---

## The research, in one line

The underlying work studies how humans project selfhood, authority, care, and institutional trust onto language models that have no stable agency — and how that weakens human oversight of AI. (This is public on the author's own grant page and essays; here it is named, not detailed.)

---

## Operating discipline — do / don't

This part survives intact, because it is method, not data — and it is one of the most reusable things in the whole system:

**Do** — keep application drafts in a saved, distilled form; prepare templates for follow-up questions; turn scattered logs into usable public outputs; prioritize self-care.

**Don't** — spray near-identical applications; panic-submit elsewhere before a response lands; send unfinished, still-hot material externally; publish dangerous specifics from a disclosure; sacrifice self-care.

The "don't" list is the accumulated judgment of what *not* to do under pressure. It is worth more than any single status update, because it is the file protecting the operator from their own urgency.

---

## The state of karmic ripening (structure, not events)

The live file keeps a running read of where things stand, in four bands. Kept here as structure, with the events removed:

- **what is starting to bear fruit** — long effort beginning to land
- **the ripening of suffering** — a loss that cannot be undone, held and not painted over
- **institutional silence** — a disclosure met with no reply, waiting
- **a new layer of practice** — understanding, bodily, that some karma cannot be resisted, and staying light anyway

Holding these four *at once*, without collapsing them into a single mood, is the work this file supports. A progress file is usually just a task list; here it is also a place to keep an honest, multi-banded read of a life in motion.

---

## Operating notes

Update on state change; archive completed items periodically; name next candidates explicitly; keep status here and out of memory; back up old versions on rename.
